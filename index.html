Case study

OrbitCV

Problem

Job seekers spend too much time formatting resumes manually. I wanted to give them a faster way to build a solid resume without fighting layout and structure for hours.

My role

I founded and built the product end to end. That included the frontend experience, CRUD flows, authentication, payments, AI resume support, deployment, and ongoing iteration.

Tech + trade-offs

I chose React and Supabase because they let me ship quickly with auth and data already in place. TanStack Query helped me clean up caching and data fetching once I realized the early UI slowness was not just a rendering issue. Paystack was the right payment choice for my context. The trade-off was taking on more state and integration complexity in exchange for moving faster with a real product.

Measurable outcome

  • Around 15–20 monthly users.
  • 25+ resumes generated.
  • 1 paid user, which gave me real validation beyond free usage.
  • Users can finish work in roughly 90 minutes instead of spending several hours building from scratch.

What I learned

Frontend performance problems are often data flow problems in disguise. Once I improved how data was fetched and cached, the product felt better immediately. I also learned how much trust clear UI and good validation create in a product tied to important life moments like job applications.

Case study

Ignited Youth App

Problem

Youth communities often rely on inconsistent reminders in chats or announcements. I wanted a cleaner product that helps users stay connected to their faith through consistent prompts and simple content delivery.

My role

I built the full product myself, from the frontend to the data layer and email notification flow.

Tech + trade-offs

I chose a web app instead of a native mobile app so I could move faster, keep the onboarding simple, and make it accessible on any device. React, Supabase, and Resend covered the core needs: UI, auth, data, and email delivery. The trade-off is that the product still needs more rollout polish and a finalized domain before a wider launch.

Measurable outcome

  • Built a working MVP with authentication and email reminders.
  • Delivered the full flow as a solo builder.
  • Rollout is active, with domain setup still in progress before wider usage tracking.

What I learned

Notification products live or die on clarity and consistency. It is not enough to send reminders; the UX has to make the habit feel simple and worth returning to.

Case study

Axion Outreach

Problem

I needed a better way to keep track of prospects and write pitches without jumping between notes, browser tabs, and scattered drafts.

My role

I designed and built the entire tool for my own workflow, which let me iterate quickly around the exact problems I wanted to solve.

Tech + trade-offs

I used React, Supabase, and the OpenAI API so I could manage prospect data and generate pitch drafts from the same interface. Because I built it for myself first, I optimized for speed and usefulness rather than broad customization. The trade-off was less external user feedback, but faster learning and faster iteration.

Measurable outcome

  • Built a single workflow for prospect tracking and client pitch generation.
  • Removed the need to manage outreach across scattered notes and tabs.
  • Usage is internal, so outcomes are practical but not formally tracked yet.

What I learned

Internal tools are one of the best ways to sharpen product instincts. You feel immediately whether a flow is helping you or slowing you down, which makes trade-offs much easier to evaluate honestly.
